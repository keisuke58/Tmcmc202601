\begin{abstract}
Biofilms are living materials whose composition, and with it their mechanical response, depends on species interactions that cannot be measured directly.
We calibrate a thermodynamically consistent multiphase model of a five-species oral biofilm, derived from the extended Hamilton principle, against \textit{in vitro} time courses of a commensal and a dysbiotic community, each under static and flow-reactor cultivation.
All 15 entries of the symmetric interaction matrix are inferred by transitional Markov chain Monte Carlo, with the stiff forward model compiled in JAX and evaluated in parallel on a GPU (about 150 times faster than a CPU).
Data enter in stages, from species composition to community viability, and each stage is checked for seed agreement, mixing and posterior mass at the prior bounds.
Root-mean-square errors of the viable species fractions are 0.06--0.09 in the four conditions; under commensal static cultivation, composition and measured live fraction cannot both be matched.
The data constrain only part of the interaction matrix: five and six entries in the two commensal conditions, all among the early colonisers and with the same signs where both determine them, and nine and eight in the dysbiotic conditions.
The coefficient between \textit{F.~nucleatum} and \textit{P.~gingivalis} is constrained in both dysbiotic conditions but not sign-determined.
The pH predicted from the calibrated composition follows the measured rise in the dysbiotic flow reactor, and removing \textit{F.~nucleatum} \textit{in silico} reduces the late increase of \textit{P.~gingivalis} about fourfold, a directly testable prediction.
\end{abstract}

%==============================================================================
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%==============================================================================

Biofilms are soft, living materials: microbial cells embedded in a self-produced matrix whose composition evolves in time and determines their mechanical response~\citep{Klempt2024ContinuumGrowth}.
On dental implants, a shift in the species composition of the biofilm from a commensal towards a dysbiotic community is associated with peri-implantitis~\citep{Szafranski2026Periimplant,Ghensi2020Implant}, and \textit{in vitro} models of peri-implant biofilms reproduce such shifts~\citep{heine2025influence}; in the ecological view of oral disease, the shift of the community, rather than a single pathogen, drives disease~\citep{Marsh2003Dental}.
Early biofilms on implant abutments \textit{in vivo} already contain disease-associated species~\citep{dieckow2024structure}.
Which interactions between the species drive such a shift is difficult to measure: co-culture experiments report how the composition changes over time, not the interactions themselves.

Continuum mechanics offers a route from composition to interaction.
Based on the extended Hamilton principle~\citep{JunkerBalzani2021ExtendedHamilton}, \citet{Klempt2025ContinuumBiofilm} derived a multiphase model in which species volume fractions and viabilities evolve under a free energy that contains an interaction matrix~$\mathbf{A}$, taken symmetric so that the interactions derive from this potential; thermodynamic consistency follows from the variational structure.
Calibrating $\mathbf{A}$ against data is an inverse characterisation problem: the physics fixes the structure of the model, and the data determine its coefficients.
Once calibrated, the model can serve as an \textit{in silico} test bed, for example for removing one species.
Bayesian updating has been used to calibrate constitutive parameters of biofilm models with surrogates~\citep{Fritsch2025BayesianMicrofilms}, but the interaction matrix poses two specific difficulties.
First, the data give 20 independent values per composition (viable and all cells) and five community viabilities for 15 unknowns, so identifiability cannot be assumed.
Second, the coupled volume-fraction and viability dynamics produce posteriors that are multimodal and bounded by the prior box, for which the convergence of standard samplers is hard to verify.

Here we infer all 15 entries of $\mathbf{A}$ from the time courses of \citet{heine2025influence} for five oral taxa---\textit{Streptococcus oralis} (So), \textit{Actinomyces naeslundii} (An), \textit{Veillonella} spp.\ (Vei), \textit{Fusobacterium nucleatum} (Fn) and \textit{Porphyromonas gingivalis} (Pg), numbered 1 to 5 in this order---in four conditions: a commensal or a dysbiotic community, each under static cultivation in well plates or dynamic cultivation in a flow reactor (HOBIC, Hannoverian Oral multispecies Biofilm Implant flow Chamber), abbreviated CS, CH, DS and DH.
We combine three elements:
first, transitional Markov chain Monte Carlo (TMCMC)~\citep{ChingChen2007TMCMC, Betz2016TMCMC} with the forward model compiled in JAX~\citep{jax2018github} and evaluated for all particles in parallel on a GPU;
second, a staged introduction of the data, from species composition to community viability; and
third, explicit acceptance criteria for every stage, including a rule for widening prior bounds, and an analysis of which interactions the data determine.
The data determine only a subset of $\mathbf{A}$, which we identify entry by entry; where the commensal conditions overlap, the identified entries agree in sign across cultivation modes, whereas the Vei--Pg coefficient of the dysbiotic community is negative in the flow reactor but close to zero under static cultivation.

%==============================================================================
\section{Model}
\label{sec:model}
%==============================================================================

At a material point, species $i=1,\dots,5$ (So, An, Vei, Fn, Pg) occupies the volume fraction $\phi_i\in(0,1)$, of which the fraction $\psi_i\in(0,1)$ is alive; the living volume fraction is $\bar\phi_i=\phi_i\psi_i$.
A void fraction $\phi_0$ closes the volume, $\sum_{l=0}^{5}\phi_l=1$.
As in \citet{Klempt2025ContinuumBiofilm}, the evolution equations result from the stationarity of the rate functional (derivation in Supplementary Section~\ref{S-sec:S_model})
\begin{equation}
\dot\Psi+\Delta+C\rightarrow\mathrm{stat}_{(\dot{\bphi},\dot{\bpsi},\gamma)},
\label{eq:stat}
\end{equation}
the local form of the extended Hamilton principle~\citep{JunkerBalzani2021ExtendedHamilton, JunkerBodeHackl2025Holistic, junker2014principle, hackl1997generalized}, with the free energy, dissipation and constraint
\begin{equation}
\Psi=-\tfrac12\,c^*\,\bar{\bphi}^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{A}\,\bar{\bphi},\qquad
\Delta=\tfrac12\,\dot{\bar{\bphi}}^{\mathsf T}\boldsymbol\eta\,\dot{\bar{\bphi}}+\tfrac12\,\dot{\bphi}^{\mathsf T}\boldsymbol\eta\,\dot{\bphi}+\tfrac12\,\dot\phi_0^2,\qquad
C=\gamma\Bigl(\sum_{l=0}^{5}\phi_l-1\Bigr).
\label{eq:potentials}
\end{equation}
Here $c^*$ is the nutrient concentration, $\boldsymbol\eta=\mathrm{diag}(\eta_i)$ with $\eta_i=1$, the last term of $\Delta$ is a unit dissipation of the void fraction $\phi_0$ that makes the system regular, and $\gamma$ is a Lagrange multiplier (no antibiotic term, as the experiments contain none).
For $n=5$ species, stationarity gives $2n+2=12$ coupled non-linear equations, for $i=1,\dots,5$,
\begin{align}
0&=-c^*\psi_i(\mathbf{A}\bar{\bphi})_i+\eta_i\bigl(\dot\phi_i\psi_i^2+\bar\phi_i\dot\psi_i+\dot\phi_i\bigr)+\gamma,\label{eq:phi}\\
0&=-c^*\phi_i(\mathbf{A}\bar{\bphi})_i+\eta_i\bigl(\dot\psi_i\phi_i^2+\bar\phi_i\dot\phi_i\bigr),\label{eq:psi}
\end{align}
together with $0=\gamma+\dot\phi_0$ and the volume constraint.
They are integrated by an implicit Euler scheme with Newton iterations (2500 steps covering the 21-day experiment), with a barrier term that diverges at 0 and 1 keeping $\phi_i,\psi_i$ inside $(0,1)$.
Only the symmetric part of $\mathbf{A}$ enters the quadratic free energy, so $\mathbf{A}$ is taken symmetric; its 15 independent entries form the parameter vector $\btheta\in\mathbb{R}^{15}$.
The coefficient $a_{ij}$ is an effective, undirected interaction of the model; it lumps mechanisms that the model does not resolve, such as metabolite exchange or co-aggregation.
Only the product $c^*a_{ij}$ enters, so we fix $c^*=25$ to keep $a_{ij}=\mathcal{O}(1)$.
Removing a species is represented by holding its volume fraction at zero.

\section{Bayesian inference}
\label{sec:inference}
%==============================================================================

\paragraph{Likelihood.}
The composition data are the species fractions of viable cells at days 3, 6, 10, 15 and 21 (day~1 is the initial condition), measured by quantitative real-time PCR after propidium monoazide (PMA) treatment~\citep{heine2025influence}, with the total-count fractions without PMA measured on the same samples (their Figs.~3A and S1).
We use the measured replicate values provided by the authors (three to five biological replicates per time point, each in technical triplicate) and the medians over them (values read off the published box plots deviate by up to 0.42; Supplementary Section~\ref{S-sec:S_raw}).
Model time is $t=s\,d$ ($s=0.2375/21$) with the day-1 composition as the initial state; starting at day~1 instead ($t=s\,(d-1)$), as the medium change after 24\,h in the flow reactor~\citep{heine2025influence} would suggest, changes the composition errors of the maximum a posteriori estimate (MAP) by at most 0.014 (0.009, 0.014, 0.010 and 0.014 in CS, CH, DS and DH; in DH the viable-composition error decreases).
The two compositions differ (root-mean-square error, RMSE, between their medians 0.101, 0.087, 0.119 and 0.087 in CS, CH, DS and DH; Supplementary Section~\ref{S-sec:S_total}) because the species differ in viability, so each is compared with its own model counterpart.
The viable fractions $y^{\mathrm{v}}_{ik}$ at time $t_k$ are compared with the living volume fractions $\hat y^{\mathrm{v}}_{ik}=\phi_i\psi_i/\sum_j\phi_j\psi_j$, the total-count fractions $y^{\mathrm{t}}_{ik}$ with $\hat y^{\mathrm{t}}_{ik}=\phi_i/\sum_j\phi_j$, and the community live fraction $\ell_k$, the fraction of membrane-intact cells from SYTO9/propidium iodide (LIVE/DEAD BacLight) staining (image based, not resolved by species; Supplementary Section~\ref{S-sec:S_raw}), with $\hat\ell_k=\sum_i\phi_i\psi_i/\sum_i\phi_i$, through the weighted Gaussian log-likelihood
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\ln p(\yobs\mid\btheta)={}&-\frac12\sum_{k}\sum_{i=1}^{5}w_{ik}\left[\frac{\bigl(y^{\mathrm{v}}_{ik}-\hat y^{\mathrm{v}}_{ik}\bigr)^2}{(\sigma^{\mathrm{v}}_{i})^2}+\frac{\bigl(y^{\mathrm{t}}_{ik}-\hat y^{\mathrm{t}}_{ik}\bigr)^2}{(\sigma^{\mathrm{t}}_{i})^2}\right]\\
&-\frac{\lambda_\psi}{2}\sum_k\frac{(\ell_k-\hat\ell_k)^2}{\sigma_\ell^2}+\mathrm{const},
\end{split}
\label{eq:lik}
\end{equation}
all evaluated at $t_k$ for parameters $\btheta$, with $\sigma^{\mathrm{v}}_{i}$ and $\sigma^{\mathrm{t}}_{i}$ the standard deviations (SD) of species $i$ estimated from the spread of the measured replicates (interquartile range/1.35 on each measurement day, at least 0.05, averaged over all six days including day~1) and $\sigma_\ell$ the SD of the live fraction between biofilms (five images per biofilm, three to nine biofilms per time point), averaged over the time points.
The weights $w_{ik}$ act on both compositions alike: they up-weight Pg ($\lambda_{\mathrm{Pg}}=5$), whose late increase is the main dynamic feature of the dysbiotic data, and the last two time points ($\lambda_{\mathrm{late}}=3$), and down-weight species with a mean fraction below 5\% ($\lambda_{\mathrm{rare}}=0.1$; Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:weights}); they change the error of each channel by at most 0.04, and without them the late Pg increase in DH is overestimated (day-21/day-15 ratio 24 against 14.9 measured; Supplementary Section~\ref{S-sec:S_lambda}).
The live fraction is weighted by $\lambda_\psi=1.5$ in the commensal conditions, 2.0 in DS and 3.0 in DH (Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:weights}).
In stages (i) and (ii) (below) the viabilities are not evolved but fixed to the values implied by the two compositions and the live fraction, $\psi_i(t_k)=(y^{\mathrm{v}}_{ik}/y^{\mathrm{t}}_{ik})\,\ell_k$ clipped to $[0.01,0.99]$ (the community value $\ell_k$ for species below 2\% of the total count; Supplementary Section~\ref{S-sec:S_psi}), and the live-fraction term is omitted; in stages (iii) and (iv) $\psi_i$ follows Eq.~\eqref{eq:psi} and all three terms are used.
The measured pH under dynamic cultivation is not used in the inference and serves as a check (Section~\ref{sec:results}).

\paragraph{Prior and stages.}
The prior is uniform on a box $[l_k,u_k]$ for each entry.
The stage-(i) boxes were set from earlier calibrations of a previous version of the model on the same data, and some of them exclude negative values or allow only small ones (CS: $a_{11}\in[0,2]$; DH: $a_{11},a_{22},a_{44}\in[0,5]$, $a_{12}\in[0.5,3]$, $a_{23}\in[0,3]$, $a_{45}\in[-0.5,6]$), so the signs of these entries are partly set by the prior in stages (i)--(ii); all of these bounds were widened in stage (iii) (Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:S_boxes}).
Inference proceeds in four stages on the same data (Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}):
(i)~composition likelihoods with $\psi_i$ fixed to the data, on the box;
(ii)~as (i), on the box narrowed to the stage-(i) posterior mean $\pm4$\,SD;
(iii)~full likelihood with $\psi_i$ evolved, back on the full box, half of the initial particles drawn around the stage-(ii) MAP and half from the prior;
(iv)~as (iii), on the box narrowed to the stage-(iii) posterior mean $\pm4$\,SD.
A CH stage-(iii) run started entirely from the prior gives the same posterior of $a_{45}$ as the warm-started seeds and a maximum log-likelihood of the same order (Supplementary Section~\ref{S-sec:S_runs}), so the warm start does not bias stage (iii).
A $\pm4$\,SD box removes less than $10^{-4}$ of a Gaussian marginal, so narrowing shortens the tempering without truncating the posterior.
If more than 20\% of the posterior mass of an entry lies in the outer 5\% of the box on one side, or this fraction grows by more than 0.10 from the previous stage, the bound is moved outwards and the stage is repeated; the boxes finally used are listed in Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:S_boxes}.

\paragraph{TMCMC on the GPU.}
TMCMC~\citep{ChingChen2007TMCMC, DelMoral2006SMC} passes a particle population through the tempered posteriors $p_m(\btheta)\propto p(\yobs\mid\btheta)^{\beta_m}p(\btheta)$, $0=\beta_0<\dots<\beta_K=1$ ($K$ tempering steps), choosing each increment so that the coefficient of variation of the importance weights stays at a target value~\citep{Betz2016TMCMC}.
After resampling, every particle performs $n_{\mathrm{mut}}$ Metropolis--Hastings steps with random-walk proposals scaled by the weighted particle covariance, alternating with differential-evolution proposals~\citep{terBraak2006DEMC} that follow strongly correlated directions; proposals outside the box are rejected without solving the model.
Each likelihood evaluation requires one solution of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:phi}--\eqref{eq:psi}, and a run requires $10^5$ to $3\times10^7$ of them.
We implement the implicit solver, including Jacobian assembly and linear solve, in JAX, compile it with XLA into a single kernel and evaluate all particles at once.
On one GPU (NVIDIA RTX\,4090) this is 170 times faster per tempering step than a 12-core CPU at matched settings (147 times end to end; Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:timing}), which makes it affordable to repeat every stage with three seeds.

\paragraph{Acceptance criteria.}
Every stage is run with three independent seeds and accepted only if
(a)~the stored log-likelihood of every particle agrees with a recomputation;
(b)~the tempering reaches $\beta=1$;
(c)~each particle is moved at least twice per tempering step and at least five times per free parameter over the run;
(d)~the maximum log-likelihood, refined by a local optimisation (L-BFGS-B) from the best particles of each seed, differs by at most 1 across seeds, i.e.\ all seeds reach the same likelihood peak; and
(e)~every posterior median differs across seeds by at most half a posterior SD; an entry whose pooled posterior SD is at least 0.8 times that of a uniform distribution on the prior box of the stage is reported as not identified and excluded from this criterion, and for an entry with several posterior modes the criterion is applied within each mode, with the mode weights reported together with their spread across seeds.
We call all other entries identified: the data constrain them beyond their prior box.
Stages (ii)--(iv) that fail are repeated with more particles or mutation steps and not interpreted; the one exception, stage (iv) of DH, is discussed in Section~\ref{sec:limits}.
Stage (i) is exploratory: it sets the stage-(ii) box and gives the evidence estimates used for model comparison (Section~\ref{sec:results}).

\section{Results and discussion}

\subsection{Fit and convergence}
Stages (ii)--(iv) of CS and CH, stages (iii)--(iv) of DS and stages (ii)--(iii) of DH meet criteria (a)--(e) with three seeds each. Stage (ii) of DS fails (d), one seed having found a higher optimum (spread 1.7); the following stages, which start half of their particles from the prior, do not inherit this. Stage (iv) of DH, on the narrowed box, meets (a)--(d) but not (e): the seed medians of $a_{13}$, $a_{22}$ and $a_{24}$ differ by up to 0.79 posterior SD, so the three seeds settle in different modes. For DH the final stage is therefore stage (iii).
None of the exploratory stage-(i) groups meets (c) (2.7--4.2 moves per free parameter) (Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:S_runs}).
At the final stage the viable composition is reproduced with an RMSE of 0.060--0.066 in CH (MAP of each of the three seeds; total-count composition 0.076--0.082, live fraction 0.11--0.12), 0.090--0.093 in CS (total-count composition 0.039--0.041, live fraction 0.23; Section~\ref{sec:limits}) 0.057 in DS (total-count composition 0.081, live fraction 0.09--0.10) and 0.073--0.076 in DH (total-count composition 0.155--0.159, live fraction 0.07--0.08) (Fig.~\ref{fig:fit}).
Accepting a stage often required more than one attempt (widened bounds in every condition, up to 20000 particles in DH; Supplementary Tables~\ref{S-tab:S_boxes} and \ref{S-tab:S_runs}); without the criteria, several of these runs would have been read as converged.

\subsection{What the data determine}
Figure~\ref{fig:ident} and Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:S_ident} classify every entry of $\mathbf{A}$ at the final stage (marginals in Supplementary Fig.~\ref{S-fig:post}); the number of directions in which the likelihood itself is narrow at the MAP is 5 in CS, 6 in CH, 6 in DS and 7 in DH, against 5, 6, 9 and 8 identified entries (Supplementary Section~\ref{S-sec:S_ident}); the counts depend on the threshold 0.8, the pattern described below does not (Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:S_thresh}). On synthetic data generated from a point of the posterior of each condition, the same pipeline reproduces this classification in 14, 15, 14 and 15 of the 15 entries of CS, CH, DS and DH (Supplementary Section~\ref{S-sec:S_recovery}).
In the commensal conditions only a few entries are identified: five in CS ($a_{11}$, $a_{22}$, $a_{33}$, $a_{13}$, $a_{23}$) and six in CH ($a_{11}$, $a_{12}$, $a_{22}$, $a_{33}$, $a_{13}$, $a_{23}$), all among the early colonisers So, An and Vei.
Where both conditions determine the sign of an entry, it agrees: the self-interactions of So and Vei are negative ($a_{11}$, 90\% intervals $[-7.0,-1.6]$ in CS and $[-1.4,-0.4]$ in CH; $a_{33}$, Section~\ref{sec:limits} and $[-5.8,-0.6]$) and the So--Vei coefficient is positive ($a_{13}$, $[+4.7,+11.3]$ and $[+0.3,+2.4]$), in line with the lactate cross-feeding between streptococci and \textit{Veillonella}~\citep{Kolenbrander2010OralMultispecies}, although $a_{13}$ is an effective coefficient and not a measurement of that mechanism.
No entry involving Fn or Pg is identified in either commensal condition, as expected for two taxa that stay below 1\% of the community from day~3 on.
In the dysbiotic conditions, where Fn and Pg reach 10--27\% of the community, more entries are identified: nine in DS and eight in DH, now including interactions of the late colonisers.
The Vei--Pg coefficient $a_{35}$ is identified in both but differs between them: negative in the flow reactor (DH, $[-20.9,-0.6]$) and close to zero under static cultivation (DS, $[-0.34,+0.24]$); the An--Fn coefficient $a_{24}$ is positive in DH ($[+1.3,+8.8]$) and not sign-determined in DS ($[-0.1,+2.8]$).
Commensal--dysbiotic comparisons are confounded by design, since the communities also differ in the \textit{Veillonella} species (\textit{V.~dispar} vs.\ \textit{V.~parvula}) and the Pg strain (DSM~20709 vs.\ W83)~\citep{heine2025influence}; the DS--DH contrast compares the same strains, although with different viability weights and prior boxes.

\subsection{The Fn--Pg coefficient}
The coefficient $a_{45}$ links the bridging organism Fn to the pathogen Pg.
Comparing the full model with a model in which $a_{45}=0$, both inferred at stage (i) with 120 mutation steps so that both groups meet the acceptance criteria, gives $\ln B=-0.06$ (CS) and $+0.03$ (CH) (Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:S_bayes}): in the commensal conditions the data do not distinguish the two models, as expected for a coefficient between two taxa below 5\%. In the dysbiotic conditions the data favour the full model, $\ln B=+7.5$ (DS) and $+2.1$ (DH), by more than the seed spread of $\ln\hat Z$.
The uniform prior penalises the full model by the log-width of the $a_{45}$ box ($\ln35\approx3.6$ in DS), so $\ln B$ is read only within a condition.
With the composition alone (stage (i)), $a_{45}$ in DH is positive in all seeds (medians $+4.7$ to $+5.1$, 90\% intervals from $[+0.2,+5.9]$ to $[+2.0,+5.9]$; $+5.2$ in the longer runs used for the Bayes factor) within the stage-(i) box, which allows only small negative values ($[-0.5,6]$).
Once the viability enters, on a box widened to $[-5,20]$ in DH, the median of $a_{45}$ stays positive in both dysbiotic conditions ($+0.6$ in DS, $+3.1$ in DH) but the 90\% intervals include zero ($[-0.2,+2.1]$ and $[-2.4,+9.2]$; Supplementary Fig.~\ref{S-fig:post}): $a_{45}$ is constrained but not sign-determined.
The composition data thus favour a positive Fn--Pg coefficient in the dysbiotic conditions, and the viability data neither confirm nor contradict it. This may reflect that \textit{S.~oralis} suppresses the Fn--Pg interaction on saliva~\citep{Kolenbrander2011Saliva}, and that in multispecies communities pairwise interactions are embedded in a wider nutritional network~\citep{Sakanaka2022Fn}.
\subsection{Checks against data not used in the inference}
A linear regression of the measured pH on the measured species fractions ($R^2=0.88$ over the continuous records of CH and DH) was fitted once and applied, unchanged, to the posterior predictive composition, so the check tests the predicted composition, not a model of pH (Fig.~\ref{fig:checks}b,c).
In DH the pH predicted in this way follows the measured rise from about 6.4 to 7.0 ($R^2=0.71$, RMSE 0.10, 85\% of the measured points inside the 90\% band; the regression applied to the measured composition itself gives RMSE 0.12). In CH the measured pH stays near 6.3; the prediction matches this level (RMSE 0.08) but not its small variations ($R^2<0$, 44\% inside the band).
The calibrated model can also be used \textit{in silico} (Fig.~\ref{fig:checks}a). With all five taxa, the day-21/day-15 ratio of Pg in DH has a posterior median of 11--13 (one value per seed; 90\% intervals up to $[0.6,32]$; measured 14.9): the model reproduces the late increase, but the data do not determine its size (Section~\ref{sec:limits}). Removing Fn lowers the ratio to 3.0 (90\% interval $[1.0,4.8]$, the same in all seeds), so the increase is reduced about fourfold but not abolished. Setting only $a_{45}=0$ removes it in most samples (median 0.8--0.9 in all seeds), so the increase rests mainly on the Fn--Pg coefficient, partly offset by the other interactions of Fn; the predicted reduction can be tested directly by a co-culture without Fn.
Under static cultivation the measured increase is smaller (ratio 2.5) and the model reproduces part of it (1.4--1.5); there, removing Fn brings the ratio to 1.0--1.1, whereas $a_{45}=0$ leaves it unchanged (1.5), so the increase in DS is carried by the other interactions of Fn rather than by $a_{45}$.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}
The likelihood treats the five species fractions as independent although they sum to one, so the posterior intervals are too narrow: in the recovery tests on synthetic data, 4 of the 6 identified entries of CH and 1 of 9 in DS, but none in CS and DH, have the true value outside their 90\% interval (Supplementary Section~\ref{S-sec:S_recovery}); the intervals of the identified entries should be read as lower bounds on their uncertainty, whereas the classification into identified and not identified is reproduced.
Unlike in CH, the compositions and the community live fraction cannot be matched at the same time in CS: raising the weight of the live fraction from $\lambda_\psi=1.5$ to 24 lowers its error from 0.23 to 0.05 but raises the error of the viable composition from 0.09 to 0.16 (deterministic optimisation on the measured data; Supplementary Section~\ref{S-sec:S_tradeoff}).
Some entries are bounded by the data on one side only: in CS the likelihood is flat for $a_{33}\lesssim-5$ and the posterior fills the box to its lower bound however far it is widened, so $a_{33}$ is reported as an upper bound ($<-5.3$ at 95\%); in DS, $a_{44}$ and $a_{13}$ ($<-2.7$ and $<-2.4$) and $a_{25}$ ($>+1.1$) are reported likewise, with 15--18\% of their posterior mass in the outer 5\% of the box.
Neither an initial state from the day-1 data nor a day-specific $\sigma_\ell$ changes this (Supplementary Section~\ref{S-sec:S_tradeoff}).
The So-dominated community that the compositions require has a live fraction above 0.9 (measured 0.65--0.77), and without antimicrobial agents the viability equation has no term that keeps part of a growing population dead; we report this tension rather than tune $\lambda_\psi$ for CS.
In DH the size of the late Pg increase is not determined by the data: the day-21/day-15 ratio is 9.6--18.0 at the MAPs of the three stage-(iii) seeds (measured 14.9) at nearly equal likelihood, its 90\% intervals reach from 0.6 to 32, and on the narrowed box of stage (iv) the seeds settle in different modes, which is why stage (iii) is final for DH.
The interaction coefficients are effective quantities of this model and data set, not direct measurements of pairwise mechanisms.
The calibration covers a 0D material point, but its dynamics are the input that continuum growth models of biofilms require~\citep{Klempt2024ContinuumGrowth}; since the likelihood uses fractions only, the absolute biomass is a model prediction whose posterior spread has to be propagated into such a coupling. At a constrained material point, growth driven by the living volume separates the conditions, whereas growth driven by the unconstrained total volume does not (Supplementary Section~\ref{S-sec:S_growth}).

%==============================================================================
\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}
%==============================================================================

The interaction matrix of a variational multiphase biofilm model was calibrated against time courses of a five-species oral community in four conditions, with a GPU-compiled forward model, a staged likelihood and explicit acceptance criteria for every stage.
Three points carry over to other inverse characterisations of living materials.
First, convergence has to be checked, not assumed: posterior mass at the prior bounds was found in every condition and disagreement between seeds in the dysbiotic ones, addressed by widening bounds and adding particles and, where it persisted (DH, stage (iv)), reported.
Second, identifiability is a result: with 20 independent values per composition and five viability values for 15 unknowns the data determine only some interactions, and stating which ones limits the biological conclusions that can be drawn from the model.
Third, a misfit can be informative: in the commensal static condition the composition and the viability data cannot be matched at once, which may point to a process the model lacks, or to a mismatch between the live/dead stain and the model viability, rather than to a failed calibration.
In the dysbiotic conditions more interactions are identified, and the Fn--Pg coefficient, which the composition data favour as positive, is constrained but not sign-determined by the full data set.
