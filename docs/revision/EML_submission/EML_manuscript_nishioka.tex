\begin{abstract}
Biofilms are living materials whose composition, and with it their mechanical response, depends on species interactions that cannot be measured directly.
We calibrate a thermodynamically consistent multiphase model of a five-species oral biofilm, derived from the extended Hamilton principle, against in-vitro time courses of a commensal and a dysbiotic community, each under static and flow-reactor cultivation.
All 15 entries of the symmetric interaction matrix are inferred by transitional Markov chain Monte Carlo, with the stiff forward model compiled in JAX and evaluated for all particles in parallel on a GPU (about 150 times faster than on a CPU).
Data enter in stages, from species composition to community viability, and each stage is checked for seed agreement, mixing and posterior mass at the prior bounds.
Root-mean-square errors of the species fractions are 0.02--0.08 in three conditions\TBD{prov.}; in the commensal static condition (0.17--0.18) composition and measured viability cannot both be matched.
The data constrain only part of the interaction matrix: four and six entries in the two commensal conditions, all among the early colonisers and with the same signs where they overlap, and eight and nine in the dysbiotic ones\TBD{prov.}.
The coefficient between \textit{F.~nucleatum} and \textit{P.~gingivalis} is constrained in both dysbiotic conditions but not sign-determined\TBD{prov.}.
The pH predicted from the calibrated composition follows the measured pH in the dysbiotic flow-reactor community (offset about 0.13 in the commensal one), and removing \textit{F.~nucleatum} in silico abolishes the late increase of \textit{P.~gingivalis}, a prediction that can be tested directly\TBD{prov.}.
\end{abstract}

%==============================================================================
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%==============================================================================

Biofilms are soft, living materials: microbial cells embedded in a self-produced matrix whose composition evolves in time and determines their mechanical response~\citep{Klempt2024ContinuumGrowth}.
On dental implants, a shift in the species composition of the biofilm from a commensal towards a dysbiotic community is associated with peri-implantitis, and in-vitro models of peri-implant biofilms reproduce such shifts~\citep{heine2025influence}; in the ecological view of oral disease, the shift of the community, rather than a single pathogen, drives disease~\citep{Marsh2003Dental}.
Early biofilms on implant abutments in vivo already contain disease-associated species~\citep{dieckow2024structure}.
Which interactions between the species drive such a shift is difficult to measure: co-culture experiments report how the composition changes over time, not the interactions themselves.

Continuum mechanics offers a route from composition to interaction.
Based on the extended Hamilton principle~\citep{JunkerBalzani2021ExtendedHamilton}, \citet{Klempt2025ContinuumBiofilm} derived a multiphase model in which species volume fractions and viabilities evolve under a free energy that contains an interaction matrix~$\mathbf{A}$, taken symmetric so that the interactions derive from this potential; thermodynamic consistency follows from the variational structure.
Calibrating $\mathbf{A}$ against data is an inverse characterization problem: the physics fixes the structure of the model, and the data determine its coefficients.
Data-driven methods for such problems are most reliable when they are guided by physics and by explicit checks of what the data can determine.
Once calibrated, the model can be used as an in-silico test bed, for example to ask how the community would develop if one species were removed.
Bayesian updating has been used to calibrate constitutive parameters of biofilm models with surrogates~\citep{Fritsch2025BayesianMicrofilms}, but the interaction matrix poses two specific difficulties.
First, five time points of five species fractions that sum to one give 20 independent composition values, plus five values of the community viability, against 15 unknowns, so identifiability cannot be assumed.
Second, the coupled volume-fraction and viability dynamics produce posteriors that are multimodal and bounded by the prior box, for which the convergence of standard samplers is hard to verify.

Here we infer all 15 entries of $\mathbf{A}$ from the time courses of \citet{heine2025influence} for five oral taxa---\textit{Streptococcus oralis} (So), \textit{Actinomyces naeslundii} (An), \textit{Veillonella} spp.\ (Vei), \textit{Fusobacterium nucleatum} (Fn) and \textit{Porphyromonas gingivalis} (Pg), numbered 1 to 5 in this order---in four conditions: a commensal or a dysbiotic community, each under static cultivation in well plates or dynamic cultivation in a flow reactor (HOBIC, Hannoverian Oral multispecies Biofilm Implant flow Chamber), abbreviated CS, CH, DS and DH (commensal/dysbiotic, static/HOBIC).
We combine three elements:
first, transitional Markov chain Monte Carlo (TMCMC)~\citep{ChingChen2007TMCMC, Betz2016TMCMC} with the forward model compiled in JAX~\citep{jax2018github} and evaluated for all particles in parallel on a GPU;
second, a staged introduction of the data, from species composition to community viability; and
third, explicit acceptance criteria for every stage, including a rule for widening prior bounds at which posterior mass accumulates, and an analysis of which interactions the data determine.
The data determine only a subset of $\mathbf{A}$, which we identify entry by entry; where the commensal conditions overlap, the identified entries agree in sign across cultivation modes, whereas the Vei--Pg coefficient of the dysbiotic community changes sign between static and dynamic cultivation\TBD{prov.}.

\section{Model}
\label{sec:model}
%==============================================================================

At a material point, species $i=1,\dots,5$ (So, An, Vei, Fn, Pg) occupies the volume fraction $\phi_i\in(0,1)$, of which the fraction $\psi_i\in(0,1)$ is alive; the living volume fraction is $\bar\phi_i=\phi_i\psi_i$.
A void fraction $\phi_0$ closes the volume, $\sum_{l=0}^{5}\phi_l=1$.
As in \citet{Klempt2025ContinuumBiofilm}, the evolution equations result from the stationarity of the rate functional (derivation in Supplementary Section~\ref{S-sec:S_model})
\begin{equation}
\dot\Psi+\Delta+C\rightarrow\mathrm{stat}_{(\dot{\bphi},\dot{\bpsi},\gamma)},
\label{eq:stat}
\end{equation}
the local form of the extended Hamilton principle~\citep{JunkerBalzani2021ExtendedHamilton, JunkerBodeHackl2025Holistic, junker2014principle, hackl1997generalized}, with the free energy, dissipation and constraint
\begin{equation}
\Psi=-\tfrac12\,c^*\,\bar{\bphi}^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{A}\,\bar{\bphi},\qquad
\Delta=\tfrac12\,\dot{\bar{\bphi}}^{\mathsf T}\boldsymbol\eta\,\dot{\bar{\bphi}}+\tfrac12\,\dot{\bphi}^{\mathsf T}\boldsymbol\eta\,\dot{\bphi},\qquad
C=\gamma\Bigl(\sum_{l=0}^{5}\phi_l-1\Bigr).
\label{eq:potentials}
\end{equation}
Here $c^*$ is the nutrient concentration, $\boldsymbol\eta=\mathrm{diag}(\eta_i)$ with $\eta_i=1$, and $\gamma$ is a Lagrange multiplier; the antibiotic term of the original model vanishes because the experiments contain no antibiotic.
For $n=5$ species, stationarity gives $2n+2=12$ coupled non-linear equations, for $i=1,\dots,5$,
\begin{align}
0&=-c^*\psi_i(\mathbf{A}\bar{\bphi})_i+\eta_i\bigl(\dot\phi_i\psi_i^2+\bar\phi_i\dot\psi_i+\dot\phi_i\bigr)+\gamma,\label{eq:phi}\\
0&=-c^*\phi_i(\mathbf{A}\bar{\bphi})_i+\eta_i\bigl(\dot\psi_i\phi_i^2+\bar\phi_i\dot\phi_i\bigr)+\gamma,\label{eq:psi}
\end{align}
together with $0=\gamma+\dot\phi_0$ and the volume constraint.
They are integrated by an implicit Euler scheme with Newton iterations (2500 steps covering the 21-day experiment), with a barrier term that diverges at 0 and 1 keeping $\phi_i,\psi_i$ inside $(0,1)$.
Only the symmetric part of $\mathbf{A}$ enters the quadratic free energy, so $\mathbf{A}$ is taken symmetric, with 15 independent entries, which form the parameter vector $\btheta\in\mathbb{R}^{15}$.
The coefficient $a_{ij}$ is an effective, undirected interaction of the model; it lumps mechanisms that the model does not resolve, such as metabolite exchange or co-aggregation.
Only the product $c^*a_{ij}$ enters, so we fix $c^*=25$ to keep $a_{ij}=\mathcal{O}(1)$.
Removing a species is represented by holding its volume fraction at zero.

\section{Bayesian inference}
\label{sec:inference}
%==============================================================================

\paragraph{Likelihood.}
The composition data are the species fractions of viable cells at days 3, 6, 10, 15 and 21 (day~1 is the initial condition), measured by quantitative real-time PCR after propidium monoazide (PMA) treatment~\citep{heine2025influence}, with the total-count fractions without PMA measured on the same samples (their Figs.~3A and S1).
We use the measured replicate values provided by the authors (three to five biological replicates per time point, each in technical triplicate) and the medians over them; values read off the published box plots deviate from these by up to 0.42 in a species fraction in the dysbiotic conditions, while the live fraction and the biofilm volume are read off to within 0.015 and a few percent (Supplementary Section~\ref{S-sec:S_raw}).
Model time is the time since inoculation, $t=s\,d$ with $s=0.2375/21$; the day-1 composition, the earliest measurement, serves as the initial state at $t=0$.
In the flow reactor the inoculated medium is replaced by sterile 1:2-diluted medium after 24\,h~\citep{heine2025influence}, so the growth phase there can also be taken to start at day~1.
Starting the model at day~1 ($t=s\,(d-1)$) changes the composition error of the maximum a posteriori estimate (MAP) by at most 0.008 in any condition without re-inference\TBD{prov.}.
The two compositions differ (root-mean-square error, RMSE, between their medians 0.101, 0.087, 0.119 and 0.087 in CS, CH, DS and DH; Supplementary Section~\ref{S-sec:S_total}) because the species differ in viability, so each is compared with its own model counterpart.
The viable fractions $y^{\mathrm{v}}_{ik}$ at time $t_k$ are compared with the living volume fractions $\hat y^{\mathrm{v}}_{ik}=\phi_i\psi_i/\sum_j\phi_j\psi_j$, the total-count fractions $y^{\mathrm{t}}_{ik}$ with $\hat y^{\mathrm{t}}_{ik}=\phi_i/\sum_j\phi_j$, and the community live fraction $\ell_k$, the fraction of membrane-intact cells from SYTO9/propidium iodide (LIVE/DEAD BacLight) staining (image based, not resolved by species; mean over 15--45 confocal images, five per biofilm, per time point, provided by the authors), with $\hat\ell_k=\sum_i\phi_i\psi_i/\sum_i\phi_i$, through the weighted Gaussian log-likelihood
\begin{equation}
\ln p(\yobs\mid\btheta)=-\frac12\sum_{k}\sum_{i=1}^{5}w_{ik}\left[\frac{\bigl(y^{\mathrm{v}}_{ik}-\hat y^{\mathrm{v}}_{ik}\bigr)^2}{(\sigma^{\mathrm{v}}_{ik})^2}+\frac{\bigl(y^{\mathrm{t}}_{ik}-\hat y^{\mathrm{t}}_{ik}\bigr)^2}{(\sigma^{\mathrm{t}}_{ik})^2}\right]-\frac{\lambda_\psi}{2}\sum_k\frac{(\ell_k-\hat\ell_k)^2}{\sigma_\ell^2}+\mathrm{const},
\label{eq:lik}
\end{equation}
all evaluated at $t_k$ for parameters $\btheta$, with $\sigma^{\mathrm{v}}_{ik}$ and $\sigma^{\mathrm{t}}_{ik}$ the standard deviations (SD) of species $i$ at time $t_k$ estimated from the spread of the measured replicates (interquartile range/1.35 over all measurements, at least 0.05) and $\sigma_\ell$ the SD of the live fraction between biofilms (five images per biofilm, three to nine biofilms per time point), averaged over the time points.
The weights $w_{ik}$ act on both compositions alike: they up-weight Pg ($\lambda_{\mathrm{Pg}}=5$), whose late increase is the main dynamic feature of the dysbiotic data, and the last two time points ($\lambda_{\mathrm{late}}=3$), and down-weight species with a mean fraction below 5\% ($\lambda_{\mathrm{rare}}=0.1$; Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:weights}); with $\lambda_{\mathrm{Pg}}=\lambda_{\mathrm{late}}=1$ ($\lambda_{\mathrm{rare}}$ unchanged) the median of $a_{45}$ in DH is unchanged but its interval widens (Supplementary Section~\ref{S-sec:S_lambda})\TBD{2c raw: redo}.
The live fraction is weighted by $\lambda_\psi=1.5$ in the commensal conditions, 2.0 in DS and 3.0 in DH (Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:weights})\TBD{$\lambda_\psi$ for raw data}.
In stages (i) and (ii) (below) the viabilities are not evolved but fixed to the values implied by the two compositions and the live fraction, $\psi_i(t_k)=(y^{\mathrm{v}}_{ik}/y^{\mathrm{t}}_{ik})\,\ell_k$ clipped to $[0.01,0.99]$ (the community value $\ell_k$ for species below 2\% of the total count; Supplementary Section~\ref{S-sec:S_psi}), and the live-fraction term is omitted; in stages (iii) and (iv) $\psi_i$ follows Eq.~\eqref{eq:psi} and all three terms are used.
The measured pH under dynamic cultivation is not used in the inference and serves as a check (Section~\ref{sec:results}).

\paragraph{Prior and stages.}
The prior is uniform on a box $[l_k,u_k]$ for each entry.
The stage-(i) boxes were set from earlier calibrations of a previous version of the model on the same data, and some of them exclude negative values or allow only small ones (CS: $a_{11}\in[0,2]$; DH: $a_{11},a_{22},a_{44}\in[0,5]$, $a_{12}\in[0.5,3]$, $a_{23}\in[0,3]$, $a_{45}\in[-0.5,6]$), so the signs of these entries are partly set by the prior in stages (i)--(ii); all of these bounds were widened in stage (iii) (Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:S_boxes}).
Inference proceeds in four stages on the same data (Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}):
(i)~composition likelihoods with $\psi_i$ fixed to the data, on the box;
(ii)~as (i), on the box narrowed to the stage-(i) posterior mean $\pm4$\,SD;
(iii)~full likelihood with $\psi_i$ evolved, back on the full box, half of the initial particles drawn around the stage-(ii) MAP and half from the prior\TBD{cold-start check, 13l};
(iv)~as (iii), on the box narrowed to the stage-(iii) posterior mean $\pm4$\,SD.
A $\pm4$\,SD box removes less than $10^{-4}$ of a Gaussian marginal, so narrowing shortens the tempering without truncating the posterior.
If more than 20\% of the posterior mass of an entry lies in the outer 5\% of the box on one side, or this fraction grows by more than 0.10 from the previous stage, the bound is moved outwards and the stage is repeated; the boxes finally used are listed in Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:S_boxes}.

\paragraph{TMCMC on the GPU.}
TMCMC~\citep{ChingChen2007TMCMC, DelMoral2006SMC} passes a particle population through the tempered posteriors $p_m(\btheta)\propto p(\yobs\mid\btheta)^{\beta_m}p(\btheta)$, $0=\beta_0<\dots<\beta_K=1$ ($K$ tempering steps), choosing each increment so that the coefficient of variation of the importance weights stays at a target value~\citep{Betz2016TMCMC}.
After resampling, every particle performs $n_{\mathrm{mut}}$ Metropolis--Hastings steps with random-walk proposals scaled by the weighted particle covariance, alternating with differential-evolution proposals~\citep{terBraak2006DEMC} that follow strongly correlated directions; proposals outside the box are rejected without solving the model.
Each likelihood evaluation requires one solution of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:phi}--\eqref{eq:psi}, and a run requires $10^5$ to $3\times10^7$ of them.
We implement the implicit solver, including Jacobian assembly and linear solve, in JAX, compile it with the XLA (Accelerated Linear Algebra) compiler into a single kernel and evaluate all particles at once with \texttt{vmap}.
On one GPU (NVIDIA RTX\,4090) this is 170 times faster per tempering step than a 12-core CPU at matched settings (147 times end to end; Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:timing}), which makes it affordable to repeat every stage with three seeds.

\paragraph{Acceptance criteria.}
Every stage is run with three independent seeds and accepted only if
(a)~the stored log-likelihood of every particle agrees with a recomputation;
(b)~the tempering reaches $\beta=1$;
(c)~each particle is moved at least twice per tempering step and at least five times per free parameter over the run;
(d)~the maximum log-likelihood, refined by a local optimisation (L-BFGS-B) from the best particles of each seed, differs by at most 1 across seeds, i.e.\ all seeds reach the same likelihood peak; and
(e)~every posterior median differs across seeds by at most half a posterior SD; an entry whose pooled posterior SD is at least 0.8 times that of a uniform distribution on the prior box of the stage is reported as not identified and excluded from this criterion, and for an entry with several posterior modes the criterion is applied within each mode, with the mode weights reported together with their spread across seeds.
We call all other entries identified: the data constrain them beyond their prior box.
Criterion~(c) counts accepted moves rather than the acceptance rate: with optimally scaled random-walk proposals~\citep{RobertsRosenthal2001Optimal}, $5d$ accepted moves displace each coordinate by about five posterior standard deviations, independently of the dimension~$d$.
Stages (ii)--(iv) that fail are repeated with more particles or mutation steps and not interpreted.
Stage (i) is exploratory; it sets the stage-(ii) box and gives the evidence estimates of TMCMC, which are used only to compare models on the same data and box and are reported with the caveat that not all stage-(i) groups meet the criteria (Section~\ref{sec:results}).

\subsection{Fit and convergence}
Stages (ii)--(iv) of CS and CH and stages (ii)--(iii) of DS meet criteria (a)--(e) with three seeds each; the DH stage-(iii) group meets (a)--(d), but its seed spread of the medians reaches 0.64 posterior SD (criterion (e): 0.5)\TBD{DH: larger run in progress; DS, DH stage (iv) pending; their numbers below are from stage (iii)}.
Of the exploratory stage-(i) groups, CS and CH do not meet (c) (4.4 and 3.8 moves per free parameter) and DS does not meet (e) (Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:S_runs}).
\TBD{2c raw: all numbers in this section.} At the final stage the composition is reproduced with an RMSE of 0.076--0.078 in CH, 0.172--0.179 in CS, 0.020--0.028 in DS and 0.081--0.083 in DH\TBD{prov.} (Fig.~\ref{fig:fit}).
Accepting a stage often required more than one attempt: posterior mass accumulated at a bound of the initial box in every condition and was released by widening that bound (Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:S_boxes}), and the dysbiotic conditions needed \TBD{18000 (DS) and 14000 (DH)} particles in stage (iii) (Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:S_runs}).

\subsection{What the data determine}
Figure~\ref{fig:ident} and Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:S_ident} classify every entry of $\mathbf{A}$ at the final stage, and Supplementary Fig.~\ref{S-fig:post} shows the marginal posteriors; the number of directions in which the likelihood itself is narrow at the MAP is 4, 4, 8 and 5, against 4, 6, 8 and 9 identified entries (Supplementary Section~\ref{S-sec:S_ident}); the counts depend on the threshold 0.8, the pattern described below does not (Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:S_thresh}).
In the commensal conditions only a few entries are identified: four in CS ($a_{11}$, $a_{22}$, $a_{33}$, $a_{13}$) and six in CH ($a_{11}$, $a_{12}$, $a_{22}$, $a_{33}$, $a_{13}$, $a_{23}$), all among the early colonisers So, An and Vei.
Where both conditions identify an entry, the sign agrees: the self-interaction of So is negative ($a_{11}$, 90\% intervals $[-4.9,-1.2]$ in CS and $[-2.5,-0.5]$ in CH) and the So--Vei coefficient is positive ($a_{13}$, $[+2.8,+9.2]$ and $[+0.01,+1.4]$), in line with the lactate cross-feeding between streptococci and \textit{Veillonella}~\citep{Kolenbrander2010OralMultispecies}, although $a_{13}$ is an effective coefficient and not a measurement of that mechanism.
Two further entries of CH, $a_{14}$ and $a_{15}$, are determined in sign only (90\% intervals $[-4.8,-0.2]$ and $[-4.8,-0.3]$), and every other entry involving Fn or Pg spreads over its whole box.
This is expected: Fn and Pg stay below 1\% of the commensal communities from day~3 on, so the composition data carry little information on their interactions.
In the dysbiotic conditions, where Fn and Pg reach 10--27\% of the community, more entries are identified: eight in DS and nine in DH, now including interactions of the late colonisers\TBD{prov.}.
The Vei--Pg coefficient $a_{35}$ is identified in both but with opposite signs, positive under static cultivation (DS, $[+0.3,+2.4]$) and negative in the flow reactor (DH, $[-13.4,-1.7]$), and the An--Fn coefficient $a_{24}$ is positive in DH ($[+2.7,+7.1]$).
A sign change between cultivation modes is a statement about the effective model under different nutrient and flow conditions, not about the pairwise mechanism itself.
Comparisons between the commensal and the dysbiotic community are confounded by design: the two communities differ not only in their ecological state but also in the \textit{Veillonella} species (\textit{V.~dispar} vs.\ \textit{V.~parvula}) and the Pg strain (DSM~20709 vs.\ W83)~\citep{heine2025influence}, so differences between their matrices combine state, strain physiology and cultivation; the DS--DH contrast above compares the same strains and is free of this confound, although the two conditions also differ in the viability weight ($\lambda_\psi=2.0$ vs.\ 3.0) and in their prior boxes.

The classification is a result in its own right: it states which conclusions about the interaction network the data can support, and it is obtained from the same runs at no additional cost.

\subsection{The Fn--Pg coefficient}
The coefficient $a_{45}$ links the bridging organism Fn to the pathogen Pg.
Comparing the full model with a model in which $a_{45}=0$, both inferred at stage (i), gives $\ln B=+0.10$ (CS), $+0.05$ (CH), $-2.67$ (DS) and $+1.94$ (DH) (Supplementary Table~\ref{S-tab:S_bayes}); because not all stage-(i) groups meet the acceptance criteria, these values are indicative.
Because the uniform prior penalises the full model by the log-width of the $a_{45}$ box, $\ln B$ is read only within a condition: in DS, where the stage-(i) box is $[-15,20]$, the penalty alone is $\ln35\approx3.6$.
In DH, the data favour a non-zero coefficient.
With the composition alone (stage (i)), $a_{45}$ in DH is positive in all seeds (90\% interval about $[+0.5,+5.5]$) within the stage-(i) box, which allows only small negative values ($[-0.5,6]$); with $\lambda_{\mathrm{Pg}}=\lambda_{\mathrm{late}}=1$ its median is unchanged but the lower 5\% bound drops to $-0.06$ to $+0.17$ across seeds (Supplementary Section~\ref{S-sec:S_lambda}).
Once the viability enters, on a box widened to $[-5,20]$ in DH, the median of $a_{45}$ stays positive in both dysbiotic conditions ($+2.8$ in DS, $+1.2$ in DH) but the 90\% intervals include zero ($[-1.3,+15.1]$ and $[-1.9,+7.5]$; Supplementary Fig.~\ref{S-fig:post})\TBD{prov.}: $a_{45}$ is constrained but not sign-determined.
Within the stage-(i) box, the composition data thus favour a positive Fn--Pg coefficient in DH, and the viability data neither confirm nor contradict it.
\subsection{Checks against data not used in the inference}
The pH measured under dynamic cultivation was not used in the inference (Fig.~\ref{fig:checks}b,c).
A linear regression of the measured pH on the measured species fractions (intercept and four fractions, Vei as reference; $R^2=0.93$ over 90 time points in CH and DH) was fitted once on the measured data and then applied, unchanged, to the posterior predictive composition, so the check tests the predicted composition, not a model of pH.
In DH the pH predicted in this way follows the measured rise from about 6.3 to 7.0 ($R^2=0.63$, RMSE 0.12)\TBD{prov.}; in CH it is offset upwards by about 0.13, because the model keeps Fn and Pg near 4--5\% where the data show below 1\%, likely a consequence of down-weighting rare species in the likelihood, and these two taxa carry the largest regression coefficients.
The calibrated model can also be used in silico: removing Fn from the dysbiotic flow-reactor community abolishes the late increase of Pg (Fig.~\ref{fig:checks}a).
The day-21/day-15 ratio of Pg falls from 2.9 (90\% interval $[2.3,3.6]$; measured 2.74) to 1.1 ($[0.3,1.8]$), and only 2--7\% of the posterior samples keep a ratio of 1.5 or more\TBD{prov.}.
Setting only $a_{45}=0$ removes the increase in most but not all samples (17--52\% keep it, depending on the seed): the effect of $a_{45}$ alone varies between seeds, whereas the effect of removing Fn is consistent across seeds.
The prediction therefore rests mainly, though not only, on the Fn--Pg coefficient; it can be tested directly by a co-culture without Fn.
Under static cultivation the model shows no comparable late increase (ratio 1.1), so the knockout is not informative there.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}
The likelihood treats the five species fractions as independent although they sum to one, so the posterior intervals may be somewhat too narrow.
\TBD{2c raw: the CS paragraph below (composition vs live fraction) is from the read-off, one-channel fit; rewrite with the new results.} The fit is poorest in CS, almost entirely in So and Vei (per-species errors 0.31 and 0.24, all others below 0.05), where the So fraction rises from 0.18 to 0.70 between day~1 and day~3.
The model can follow this rise: a deterministic optimisation of the composition error alone, started from the MAP with every entry in $[-15,20]$, reaches an error of 0.075, but only with a live fraction near 0.97, against the measured 0.65--0.77 (Supplementary Section~\ref{S-sec:S_ceiling}).
The composition and the live/dead staining cannot both be matched in CS, and the posterior settles on a compromise; we report it rather than re-weight the channels for this condition.
The compromise is sharpest at the MAP: the bulk of the CS posterior lies closer to the composition data (pointwise posterior median, error 0.125), whereas the MAP, nearly identical in all three seeds (composition error 0.172--0.179), sits where the live fraction is matched best; in the other conditions the two differ by less than 0.02 (Supplementary Section~\ref{S-sec:S_seedmap}).
The community live fraction does not determine the species-specific viabilities $\psi_i$ (in the model they span 0.03--0.96), so the living fractions of individual species are not predictions of the calibrated model; only the community live fraction is (Supplementary Fig.~\ref{S-fig:S_growth}a). Viable and total-count compositions used as separate channels would constrain $\psi_i$ species by species (Supplementary Section~\ref{S-sec:S_psi}).
The composition data are viable-cell fractions; the model fractions are about as close to the total-count data (Supplementary Section~\ref{S-sec:S_total}).
The interaction coefficients are effective quantities of this model and data set; they lump mechanisms that the model does not resolve, such as metabolite exchange and pH, and are not direct measurements of pairwise mechanisms.
The calibration covers a 0D material point; the spatial structure of the biofilm and its mechanics are not part of the inference.
The calibrated dynamics are, however, the input that continuum growth models of biofilms require~\citep{Klempt2024ContinuumGrowth}: the total volume fraction of the point model can drive the growth variable of a finite-element model, so that differences in growth-induced stress between conditions follow from the calibrated interactions rather than from assumed species-specific growth weights.
Because the likelihood uses species fractions only, the absolute amount of biomass is a prediction of the model rather than a calibrated quantity, and its posterior spread has to be propagated into such a coupling.
At a material point constrained in all directions, growth driven by the living volume separates the conditions, whereas growth driven by the total volume, which the data do not constrain, does not (Supplementary Section~\ref{S-sec:S_growth}).

%==============================================================================
\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}
%==============================================================================

The interaction matrix of a variational multiphase biofilm model was calibrated against time courses of a five-species oral community in four conditions, with a GPU-compiled forward model, a staged likelihood and explicit acceptance criteria for every stage.
Three points carry over to other inverse characterizations of living materials.
First, convergence has to be checked, not assumed: posterior mass at the prior bounds was found in every condition and disagreement between seeds in the dysbiotic ones, and both were addressed only by widening bounds and adding particles.
Second, identifiability is a result: with 20 independent composition values and five viability values for 15 unknowns the data determine only some interactions, and stating which ones limits the biological conclusions that can be drawn from the model.
Third, a misfit can be informative: in the commensal static condition the composition and the viability data cannot be matched at once, which may point to a process the model lacks, or to a mismatch between the live/dead stain and the model viability, rather than to a failed calibration.
In the dysbiotic conditions more interactions are identified, and the Fn--Pg coefficient, which the composition data favour as positive in DH within the stage-(i) box, is constrained but not sign-determined by the full data set\TBD{prov.}.
