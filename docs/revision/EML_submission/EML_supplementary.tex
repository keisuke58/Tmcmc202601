\section{Model derivation}
\label{sec:S_model}

\subsection*{State variables and kinematics}

Following \citet{Klempt2025ContinuumBiofilm, Klempt2024ContinuumGrowth}, the biofilm is described at a material point by two sets of internal variables: the volume fraction $\phi_i \in (0,1)$ occupied by species~$i$ ($i=1,\dots,n$; here $n=5$), and the viability fraction $\psi_i \in (0,1)$ representing the proportion of living cells within species~$i$.
The living volume fraction is then
\begin{equation}
\bar{\phi}_i := \phi_i\,\psi_i,
\qquad i=1,\dots,n.
\label{eq:living}
\end{equation}
An additional void fraction~$\phi_0$ closes the system via the holonomic constraint
\begin{equation}
\sum_{l=0}^{n}\phi_l(t) - 1 = 0 \quad\forall\,t.
\label{eq:constraint}
\end{equation}



\subsection*{Variational formulation}
The evolution equations result from the stationarity of the rate functional \citep{junker2014principle, hackl1997generalized}, the local form of the extended Hamilton principle \citep{JunkerBalzani2021ExtendedHamilton, JunkerBodeHackl2025Holistic}:
\begin{equation}
    \dot{\Psi} + \Delta + C \rightarrow \mathrm{stat}_{(\dot{\boldsymbol{\xi}}, \, \gamma)},
    \label{eq:variational_local}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\xi} = (\boldsymbol{\phi}, \boldsymbol{\psi})^T$ collects the volume fractions and viability fractions, $\gamma$ is a Lagrange multiplier enforcing the volume constraint, $C$ is the constraint function, and $\Delta$ the dissipation function. The free energy density encodes nutrient-driven growth and antibiotic-induced killing:
\begin{equation}
\Psi = -\tfrac{1}{2}\,c^*\,\bar{\bphi}^T \mathbf{A}\,\bar{\bphi}
      + \tfrac{1}{2}\,\alpha^*\,\bpsi^T \mathbf{B}\,\bpsi,
\label{eq:psi}
\end{equation}
where $c^*$ is the nutrient concentration, $\alpha^*$ the antibiotic concentration, $\mathbf{A}\in\Real^{n\times n}$ the symmetric growth coefficient matrix, and $\mathbf{B}=\mathrm{diag}(b_1,\dots,b_n)$ the antibiotic sensitivity matrix.

The dissipation function is derived from the rate-dependent potential~\citep{Klempt2025ContinuumBiofilm}:
\begin{equation}
\Delta = \tfrac{1}{2}\,\dot{\bar{\bphi}}^T\boldsymbol{\eta}\,\dot{\bar{\bphi}}
          + \tfrac{1}{2}\,\dot{\bphi}^T\boldsymbol{\eta}\,\dot{\bphi},
\label{eq:dissipation}
\end{equation}
with the diagonal viscosity matrix $\boldsymbol{\eta}=\mathrm{diag}(\eta_1,\dots,\eta_n)$. The first term couples $\dot\phi_i$ and $\dot\psi_i$ through $\dot{\bar\phi}_i = \dot\phi_i\psi_i + \phi_i\dot\psi_i$, giving rise to non-linear dissipative interactions; the second term regularises the system to full rank. Following \citet{Klempt2025ContinuumBiofilm}, the viscosity parameters are set to $\eta_i=1$ for all species, rendering them dimensionless scaling factors; the physical time scale is absorbed into the time-step size $\Delta t$.

The volume constraint~\eqref{eq:constraint} enters through a Lagrange multiplier~$\gamma$:
\begin{equation}
C = \gamma\Bigl(\sum_{l=0}^{n}\phi_l - 1\Bigr).
\label{eq:cfunc}
\end{equation}


\subsection*{Stationarity conditions}


Evaluating the stationarity conditions yields the coupled evolution equations~\citep{Klempt2025ContinuumBiofilm}:

\medskip\noindent
\textit{Volume fraction} ($i=1,\dots,n$):
\begin{equation}
0 = -c^*\,\psi_i\,\bigl(\mathbf{A}\bar{\bphi}\bigr)_i
    + \eta_i\bigl(\dot\phi_i\,\psi_i^2 + \bar\phi_i\,\dot\psi_i + \dot\phi_i\bigr)
    + \gamma,
\label{eq:phi}
\end{equation}

\noindent\textit{Viability fraction} ($i=1,\dots,n$):
\begin{equation}
0 = -c^*\,\phi_i\,\bigl(\mathbf{A}\bar{\bphi}\bigr)_i
    + \alpha^*\,b_i\,\psi_i
    + \eta_i\bigl(\dot\psi_i\,\phi_i^2 + \bar\phi_i\,\dot\phi_i\bigr)
    + \gamma,
\label{eq:psi_evol}
\end{equation}

\noindent\textit{Void fraction}:
\begin{equation}
0 = \gamma + \dot\phi_0,
\label{eq:phi0}
\end{equation}

\noindent\textit{Volume constraint}:
\begin{equation}
0 = \sum_{l=0}^{n}\phi_l - 1,
\label{eq:constraint_eq}
\end{equation}
In the numerical implementation, a barrier penalty $\Psi_{\mathrm{bar}}=K_p\bigl[\sum_{i=0}^{n}\phi_i^{-2}(1-\phi_i)^{-2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\psi_i^{-2}(1-\psi_i)^{-2}\bigr]$ with $K_p=\eta_i\times10^{-4}$, which diverges at 0 and 1, is added to enforce the box constraints $\phi_i,\psi_i\in(0,1)$~\citep{Klempt2025ContinuumBiofilm}; its contribution is omitted from Eqs.~\eqref{eq:phi}--\eqref{eq:phi0} for clarity.
The initial state is the day-1 composition scaled to $\sum_i\phi_i(0)=1-10^{-6}$, so that the void fraction starts at $\phi_0(0)=1-\sum_i\phi_i(0)\approx0$, and all species start fully viable, $\psi_i(0)=0.999$; the barrier keeps the state inside $(0,1)$ from the first time step on (e.g.\ $\phi_0\approx3\times10^{-9}$ after one step).
The system~\eqref{eq:phi}--\eqref{eq:constraint_eq} constitutes $2n+2$ coupled non-linear equations for the unknowns $(\phi_1,\dots,\phi_n,\phi_0,\psi_1,\dots,\psi_n,\gamma)$, solved at each time step via Newton's method with an implicit Euler discretisation ($\Delta t = 10^{-4}$, $N_{\mathrm{step}}=2{,}500$ steps, $t_{\max}=0.25$; day~$d$ is mapped to $t=0.2375\,d/21$, so day~21 lies at 95\% of the integration window).

Only the symmetric part of $\mathbf{A}$ enters the quadratic free energy, so $\mathbf{A}$ is taken symmetric, $a_{ij}=a_{ji}$, and the interactions derive from this potential; the variational derivation ensures thermodynamic consistency~\citep{JunkerBalzani2021ExtendedHamilton}.
Since $c^*$ enters the evolution equations only through the product $c^*a_{ij}$, its absolute value is non-identifiable; we fix $c^*=25$ so that the inferred $a_{ij}$ remain $\mathcal{O}(1)$.
The Heine et al.\ experiments do not involve antibiotic agents, so we set $\alpha^*=0$, which eliminates the decay term $\alpha^*b_i\psi_i$ from Eq.~\eqref{eq:psi_evol}; the antibiotic sensitivity matrix~$\mathbf{B}$ is therefore inactive and excluded from estimation.
The symmetric interaction matrix~$\mathbf{A}$ has 15 independent entries, with species numbered 1~So, 2~An, 3~Vei, 4~Fn, 5~Pg; these form the 15-dimensional parameter vector~$\btheta$.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Staged GPU-accelerated TMCMC (one seed; every stage is run with three seeds and checked as in the main text)}
\label{alg:tmcmc}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Composition data $\yobs$, viability data, $N_p$ particles, GPU device
\ENSURE MAP estimate $\hat{\btheta}^{\mathrm{MAP}}$, posterior samples, log-evidence $\ln\hat{Z}$
\STATE $\triangleright$ \textbf{Stage (i): viable and total-count composition, $\psi_i$ fixed to the data, initial box}
\STATE Draw $\{\btheta_j\}_{j=1}^{N_p}$ from the stage prior; set $\beta_0\leftarrow 0$
\WHILE{$\beta_m < 1$}
    \STATE Find $\beta_{m+1}$ s.t.\ $\mathrm{CoV}[\{w_j\}]=\delta_{\mathrm{target}}$ via bisection
    \STATE Compute weights $w_j \leftarrow p(\yobs\mid\btheta_j)^{\beta_{m+1}-\beta_m}$
    \STATE Resample $N_p$ particles $\propto w_j$ (systematic)
    \STATE Estimate weighted covariance $\hat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}^{(m)}$ (weighted particle covariance)
    \FOR{$k = 1$ \TO $n_{\mathrm{mut}}$}
        \STATE Propose $\btheta_{j}'$ for all $j$ (random-walk or differential-evolution move)
        \STATE Evaluate all $N_p$ likelihoods in parallel via \texttt{jax.vmap} on GPU
        \STATE Accept or reject (Metropolis--Hastings); reject proposals outside the box
    \ENDFOR
\ENDWHILE
\STATE $K\leftarrow m$ (number of tempering steps)
\STATE Record MAP and posterior standard deviation (SD) per parameter
\STATE $\triangleright$ \textbf{Stage (ii): composition likelihood, box mean $\pm4$\,SD}
\STATE Repeat lines 2--14 on the narrowed box, starting from the previous particles
\STATE $\triangleright$ \textbf{Stages (iii)--(iv): add the viability channel; initial box, then mean $\pm4$\,SD}
\STATE Evolve $\psi_i$ and add the weighted live-fraction channel (Table~\ref{tab:weights})
\STATE Repeat lines 2--14 for each stage
\RETURN $\hat{\btheta}^{\mathrm{MAP}}$, posterior samples, $\ln\hat{Z}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Identifiability of the interaction entries}
\label{sec:S_ident}
Table~\ref{tab:S_ident} lists, for the final stage (iv) of every condition (stage (iii) for DH\TBD{DS: stage to be fixed}), the median and the 90\% interval of each entry, pooled over the three seeds.
The reference box is the prior box of that stage (Table~\ref{tab:S_boxes}, column (iv); for DS and DH column (iii)).
An entry whose pooled posterior SD is at least 0.8 times that of a uniform distribution on this box is shown in grey and excluded from the seed-agreement criterion: the data do not constrain its magnitude beyond the prior box. Among these, entries whose 90\% interval excludes zero are marked s.o.\ (sign only), the others n.i.\ (not identified). All other entries are called identified (constrained by the data); an identified entry need not be sign-determined (e.g.\ $a_{45}$ in DS and DH).
Where the stage-(iv) box was narrowed to the stage-(iii) mean $\pm4$\,SD, the uniform SD on that box is about 2.3 stage-(iii) SDs; for these entries the classification is in effect made against the stage-(iii) box.

The threshold 0.8 is a convention. Table~\ref{tab:S_thresh} counts the identified entries for thresholds between 0.5 and 0.9.
The counts change, most in the commensal conditions, where some entries lie close to 0.8 (CS: 0.77 and 0.81; CH: 0.73 and 0.83), whereas DS has no entry between 0.71 and 0.93.
The pattern does not change: at every threshold the dysbiotic conditions have at least as many identified entries as the commensal ones, and the entries identified in the commensal conditions are interactions of So, An and Vei; at 0.9 they also include So--Fn and So--Pg, the two entries that are sign-determined in CH.

\paragraph{Curvature of the likelihood.}
As a check that does not use the posterior samples, we computed the Hessian $\mathbf{H}$ of $-\ln p(\yobs\mid\btheta)$ at the MAP over the 15 free entries, scaled each entry by the standard deviation of a uniform distribution on its box, and counted the eigen-directions with scaled curvature of at least $1/0.8^2$, i.e.\ directions in which the likelihood is narrower than the identification threshold.
Their number is 5 in CS, 6 in CH and 7 in DH, against 5, 6 and 8 entries identified from the posterior\TBD{DS after its final stage; old values 8 against 8}.
The counts agree in CS and CH; in DH the posterior identifies one entry more than the local curvature, because entries can be constrained jointly along correlated directions and by the non-quadratic shape of the likelihood, which a single Hessian at the MAP does not capture.
The Hessian is evaluated at the final MAP (locally optimised in CS and CH, the best particle in DH); it is not positive definite at any of them (two, one and four negative eigenvalues in CS, CH and DH), so entry-wise Laplace intervals are not reported.

\section{Effect of the likelihood weights}
\label{sec:S_lambda}
The weights of the likelihood (main text, Section~3) encode two choices: the composition terms up-weight Pg ($\lambda_{\mathrm{Pg}}=5$), whose late increase is the main dynamic feature of the dysbiotic data, and the last two time points ($\lambda_{\mathrm{late}}=3$), and down-weight species with a mean fraction below 5\% ($\lambda_{\mathrm{rare}}=0.1$); the live-fraction term is weighted by $\lambda_\psi$ (Table~\ref{tab:weights}).
To see how much the fit depends on these choices, the two-channel log-likelihood was maximised for each condition with three sets of weights: the current ones (A), no weights ($\lambda_{\mathrm{Pg}}=\lambda_{\mathrm{late}}=\lambda_{\mathrm{rare}}=1$, B) and $\lambda_\psi=2$ in all conditions (C).
Each maximisation starts from the MAP of stage~(iii) (the seed with the highest log-likelihood) and uses the stage-(iii) box (L-BFGS-B, at most 150 iterations, measured data); it is a deterministic check of where the optimum moves, not a repeated sampling.
Table~\ref{tab:S_lambda} lists the fit of the three channels, $a_{45}$ and the Pg ratio of day~21 to day~15 at the optimum (viable fractions, as in the main text).
The weights change the error of each channel by at most 0.04 within a condition.
In CS the live-fraction error stays at 0.23 under all three sets, so the conflict between composition and live fraction (Section~\ref{sec:S_tradeoff}) is not caused by the weights.
The sign of $a_{45}$ at the optimum does not change with the weights in CS, CH and DH (positive in CS and DH, negative in CH); in DS it does ($+0.24$ with the current weights, $-0.93$ without), consistent with $a_{45}$ not being sign-determined there. In the commensal conditions $a_{45}$ is not identified (its posterior spans the box), so its value at the optimum carries little information.
In DH the up-weighting of Pg and of the late time points matters for the late Pg increase: the day-21/day-15 ratio is 16.9 with the current weights and 15.8 with $\lambda_\psi=2$, against 14.9 measured, but 24.1 without weights; in DS, which shows no comparable late increase, the ratio stays at 1.5--1.6 under all three sets.

\section{Species-resolved viability implied by the data}
\label{sec:S_psi}
The viable and the total-count fractions together imply a viability for each species, $\psi_i^{\mathrm{data}}=(y_i^{\mathrm{viable}}/y_i^{\mathrm{total}})\,\ell$, where $y_i$ are the normalised fractions and $\ell$ is the community live fraction (medians over the measured replicates and mean over the images; days with a total-count fraction below 2\% are left out).
Resampling the biological replicates of both compositions and the biofilms of the live fraction (five images per biofilm; bootstrap) gives wide intervals: the 90\% interval of $\psi_i^{\mathrm{data}}$ on a single day has a median width of 0.57 and its upper end exceeds 1 for 45\% of the species and days, so $\psi_i^{\mathrm{data}}$ enters only stages (i)--(ii) as fixed viabilities and serves as a check, not as data in the likelihood.
Table~\ref{tab:S_psi} compares it with the viability $\psi_i$ of the final MAP.
With the viable and the total-count compositions as separate channels of the likelihood, $\psi_i$ is constrained species by species, but only through the products $\phi_i\psi_i$ of the viable composition and through the community live fraction; for a species that makes up a small part of the community both constrain $\psi_i$ weakly.
In CH the model reproduces the viability of the dominant species So (0.37 against 0.35 in the data), while An and Vei stay near 0.9 (data 0.24 and 0.53, whose single-day intervals are wide; see above).
In CS the model viabilities of So, An and Vei are 0.96--0.97 against 0.50--0.80 in the data; this is the tension of CS described in Section~4.5 of the main text and in Section~\ref{sec:S_tradeoff}: the model has no term that keeps part of a growing population dead, so the community it needs for the compositions is almost fully viable.
In both commensal conditions Fn and Pg are mostly dead in the model (0.05--0.11), in line with the one value available for Fn in CS (0.09); on the other days and for Pg their total-count fraction is below 2\% and $\psi_i^{\mathrm{data}}$ is not defined.

\section{Composition against live fraction in CS}
\label{sec:S_tradeoff}
To test whether the CS tension depends on the weights, we minimised the negative log-likelihood of the main text (measured data, two compositions and the live fraction, biofilm-level $\sigma_\ell$) with L-BFGS-B, started from the previous MAP, for $\lambda_\psi=1.5$, 3, 6, 12 and 24 (Fig.~\ref{fig:S_tradeoff}).
The errors move along a single curve: no weight brings both the composition and the live-fraction errors below 0.1.
The same holds with the initial state taken from the day-1 data (total-count composition, species viabilities from the data; live-fraction error 0.237 against 0.231) and with a day-specific $\sigma_\ell$ (the between-biofilm SD of each day, at least 0.03), which moves CS along the same curve (live fraction 0.18, viable composition 0.12, total-count composition 0.09).
In CH the same procedure gives errors of 0.07 (viable), 0.07 (total) and 0.12 (live fraction) at the default weight.
A death term does not remove the tension either. Without antimicrobial agents the term $\alpha^*b_i\psi_i$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:psi_evol} is inactive; switching it on ($\alpha^*=1$, $b_i\in[0,10]$ added to the parameters) lowers the CS live-fraction error only from 0.23 to 0.22, although it brings the model viabilities closer to those implied by the data (error 0.36 to 0.27; in CH 0.45 to 0.25, where the live-fraction error falls from 0.12 to 0.08). The death rates do not vanish in the flow-reactor condition CH, so the data do not single out the static cultures as the ones that need such a term. Whether the extra parameters are supported by the data would require a model comparison (Bayes factor), which we leave to future work; all results in the main text use $\alpha^*=0$.

\section{Composition-only fit in CS}
\label{sec:S_ceiling}
To separate a limitation of the model from the conflict between the composition and live-fraction channels in CS, we minimised the negative log-likelihood of the main text with the live-fraction channel switched off ($\lambda_\psi=0$; viable and total-count compositions kept, measured data), with L-BFGS-B started from the MAP and from two random perturbations of it, and with every free entry restricted to $[-15,20]$.
The best solution reaches a viable-composition error of 0.078 and a total-count error of 0.042, against 0.090 and 0.039 at the MAP; the three starts end at 0.078--0.082.
Its live-fraction error rises from 0.23 to 0.28.
Dropping the live fraction altogether therefore improves the viable composition by only about 0.01: the remaining composition error in CS is a limit of the model, not a cost of fitting the live fraction.
This is a deterministic diagnostic, not a posterior; its solution lies near the edge of the widened range ($a_{13}=18.7$). Generated by \texttt{tools/two\_channel\_pilot\_v2.py} (options in the archived output \texttt{CS\_componly.json}).

\section{Growth-induced stress at a constrained material point}
\label{sec:S_growth}
To show how the calibration enters a mechanical model, the posterior dynamics of each condition drive the isotropic growth law of the continuum model of \citet{Klempt2024ContinuumGrowth}: $\mathbf{F}_g=(1+\alpha)\mathbf{I}$ with $\mathrm{d}\alpha/\mathrm{d}T^\ast=k_\alpha\varphi_g$, where $T^\ast$ is time normalised by the length of the experiment (21 days) and $k_\alpha=10^{-3}$ as in that work.
At a material point that is constrained in all directions, $\mathbf{F}=\mathbf{I}$, the elastic part is $\mathbf{F}_e=\mathbf{I}/(1+\alpha)$ with $J_e=(1+\alpha)^{-3}$, the deviatoric stress vanishes and the Cauchy stress is $\boldsymbol{\sigma}=K(J_e-1)\mathbf{I}$, with $K=E/[3(1-2\nu)]$, $E=E_0(\varphi_{\mathrm{tot}}^2+f)$, $\nu=0.49$ and $f=10^{-3}$, so no finite-element solution is needed.
We report the growth pressure $p=-\tfrac13\operatorname{tr}\boldsymbol{\sigma}$ relative to $E_0$ for two choices of the driver $\varphi_g$: the living volume $\sum_i\varphi_i\psi_i$, whose community fraction is constrained by the live/dead data, and the total volume $\varphi_{\mathrm{tot}}=\sum_i\varphi_i$, which the likelihood does not constrain (it uses fractions only).
Driven by the total volume, the conditions are hardly distinguishable: $\varphi_{\mathrm{tot}}$ stays at 0.94--0.98 and $p/E_0$ on day~21 is 0.042 in CH, 0.044 in DH and 0.046 in CS (Fig.~\ref{fig:S_growth}b, dashed)\TBD{DS; old: 0.042--0.046 in all four}.
Driven by the living volume, they separate: $p/E_0$ on day~21 is 0.023 (90\% interval $[0.020,0.025]$) in CH, 0.024 ($[0.023,0.025]$) in DH and 0.041 ($[0.038,0.043]$) in CS\TBD{DS}.
The high CS value follows the CS live fraction of the model (0.93 on day~21), which lies above the measured 0.65--0.77 (Section~\ref{sec:S_tradeoff}).
This is an illustration of the coupling, not a prediction of the stress in a biofilm: $k_\alpha$ and $E_0$ are taken from another system and $p$ scales linearly with both, and a spatial model adds nutrient transport, boundary conditions and the spatial distribution of the species.
It shows that the dependence of the growth stress on the condition enters through the calibrated viability rather than through assumed species-specific growth parameters, and that the posterior spread can be propagated to the stress.
