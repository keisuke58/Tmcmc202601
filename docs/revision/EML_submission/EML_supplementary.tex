\section{Model derivation}
\label{sec:S_model}

\subsection*{State variables and kinematics}

Following \citet{Klempt2025ContinuumBiofilm, Klempt2024ContinuumGrowth}, the biofilm is described at a material point by two sets of internal variables: the volume fraction $\phi_i \in (0,1)$ occupied by species~$i$ ($i=1,\dots,n$; here $n=5$), and the viability fraction $\psi_i \in (0,1)$ representing the proportion of living cells within species~$i$.
The living volume fraction is then
\begin{equation}
\bar{\phi}_i := \phi_i\,\psi_i,
\qquad i=1,\dots,n.
\label{eq:living}
\end{equation}
An additional void fraction~$\phi_0$ closes the system via the holonomic constraint
\begin{equation}
\sum_{l=0}^{n}\phi_l(t) - 1 = 0 \quad\forall\,t.
\label{eq:constraint}
\end{equation}



\subsection*{Variational formulation}
The evolution equations result from the stationarity of the rate functional \citep{junker2014principle, hackl1997generalized}, the local form of the extended Hamilton principle \citep{JunkerBalzani2021ExtendedHamilton, JunkerBodeHackl2025Holistic}:
\begin{equation}
    \dot{\Psi} + \Delta + C \rightarrow \mathrm{stat}_{(\dot{\boldsymbol{\xi}}, \, \gamma)},
    \label{eq:variational_local}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\xi} = (\boldsymbol{\phi}, \boldsymbol{\psi})^T$ collects the volume fractions and viability fractions, $\gamma$ is a Lagrange multiplier enforcing the volume constraint, $C$ is the constraint function, and $\Delta$ the dissipation function. The free energy density encodes nutrient-driven growth and antibiotic-induced killing:
\begin{equation}
\Psi = -\tfrac{1}{2}\,c^*\,\bar{\bphi}^T \mathbf{A}\,\bar{\bphi}
      + \tfrac{1}{2}\,\alpha^*\,\bpsi^T \mathbf{B}\,\bpsi,
\label{eq:psi}
\end{equation}
where $c^*$ is the nutrient concentration, $\alpha^*$ the antibiotic concentration, $\mathbf{A}\in\Real^{n\times n}$ the symmetric growth coefficient matrix, and $\mathbf{B}=\mathrm{diag}(b_1,\dots,b_n)$ the antibiotic sensitivity matrix.

The dissipation function is derived from the rate-dependent potential~\citep{Klempt2025ContinuumBiofilm}:
\begin{equation}
\Delta = \tfrac{1}{2}\,\dot{\bar{\bphi}}^T\boldsymbol{\eta}\,\dot{\bar{\bphi}}
          + \tfrac{1}{2}\,\dot{\bphi}^T\boldsymbol{\eta}\,\dot{\bphi},
\label{eq:dissipation}
\end{equation}
with the diagonal viscosity matrix $\boldsymbol{\eta}=\mathrm{diag}(\eta_1,\dots,\eta_n)$. The first term couples $\dot\phi_i$ and $\dot\psi_i$ through $\dot{\bar\phi}_i = \dot\phi_i\psi_i + \phi_i\dot\psi_i$, giving rise to non-linear dissipative interactions; the second term regularises the system to full rank. Following \citet{Klempt2025ContinuumBiofilm}, the viscosity parameters are set to $\eta_i=1$ for all species, rendering them dimensionless scaling factors; the physical time scale is absorbed into the time-step size $\Delta t$.

The volume constraint~\eqref{eq:constraint} enters through a Lagrange multiplier~$\gamma$:
\begin{equation}
C = \gamma\Bigl(\sum_{l=0}^{n}\phi_l - 1\Bigr).
\label{eq:cfunc}
\end{equation}


\subsection*{Stationarity conditions}


Evaluating the stationarity conditions yields the coupled evolution equations~\citep{Klempt2025ContinuumBiofilm}:

\medskip\noindent
\textit{Volume fraction} ($i=1,\dots,n$):
\begin{equation}
0 = -c^*\,\psi_i\,\bigl(\mathbf{A}\bar{\bphi}\bigr)_i
    + \eta_i\bigl(\dot\phi_i\,\psi_i^2 + \bar\phi_i\,\dot\psi_i + \dot\phi_i\bigr)
    + \gamma,
\label{eq:phi}
\end{equation}

\noindent\textit{Viability fraction} ($i=1,\dots,n$):
\begin{equation}
0 = -c^*\,\phi_i\,\bigl(\mathbf{A}\bar{\bphi}\bigr)_i
    + \alpha^*\,b_i\,\psi_i
    + \eta_i\bigl(\dot\psi_i\,\phi_i^2 + \bar\phi_i\,\dot\phi_i\bigr)
    + \gamma,
\label{eq:psi_evol}
\end{equation}

\noindent\textit{Void fraction}:
\begin{equation}
0 = \gamma + \dot\phi_0,
\label{eq:phi0}
\end{equation}

\noindent\textit{Volume constraint}:
\begin{equation}
0 = \sum_{l=0}^{n}\phi_l - 1,
\label{eq:constraint_eq}
\end{equation}
In the numerical implementation, a logarithmic barrier penalty $\Psi_{\mathrm{bar}}$ with $K_p=\eta_i\times10^{-4}$ is added to enforce the box constraints $\phi_i,\psi_i\in(0,1)$~\citep{Klempt2025ContinuumBiofilm}; its contribution is omitted from Eqs.~\eqref{eq:phi}--\eqref{eq:phi0} for clarity.
The system~\eqref{eq:phi}--\eqref{eq:constraint_eq} constitutes $2n+2$ coupled non-linear equations for the unknowns $(\phi_1,\dots,\phi_n,\phi_0,\psi_1,\dots,\psi_n,\gamma)$, solved at each time step via Newton's method with an implicit Euler discretisation ($\Delta t = 10^{-4}$, $N_{\mathrm{step}}=2{,}500$ steps, $t_{\max}=0.25$; day~$d$ is mapped to $t=0.2375\,d/21$, so day~21 lies at 95\% of the integration window).

Only the symmetric part of $\mathbf{A}$ enters the quadratic free energy, so $\mathbf{A}$ is taken symmetric, $a_{ij}=a_{ji}$, and the interactions derive from this potential; the variational derivation ensures thermodynamic consistency~\citep{JunkerBalzani2021ExtendedHamilton}.
Since $c^*$ enters the evolution equations only through the product $c^*a_{ij}$, its absolute value is non-identifiable; we fix $c^*=25$ so that the inferred $a_{ij}$ remain $\mathcal{O}(1)$.
The Heine et al.\ experiments do not involve antibiotic agents, so we set $\alpha^*=0$, which eliminates the decay term $\alpha^*b_i\psi_i$ from Eq.~\eqref{eq:psi_evol}; the antibiotic sensitivity matrix~$\mathbf{B}$ is therefore inactive and excluded from estimation.
The symmetric interaction matrix~$\mathbf{A}$ has 15 independent entries, with species numbered 1~So, 2~An, 3~Vei, 4~Fn, 5~Pg; these form the 15-dimensional parameter vector~$\btheta$.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Staged GPU-accelerated TMCMC (one seed; every stage is run with three seeds and checked as in the main text)}
\label{alg:tmcmc}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Composition data $\yobs$, viability data, $N_p$ particles, GPU device
\ENSURE MAP estimate $\hat{\btheta}^{\mathrm{MAP}}$, posterior samples, log-evidence $\ln\hat{Z}$
\STATE $\triangleright$ \textbf{Stage (i): composition likelihood, initial box}
\STATE Draw $\{\btheta_j\}_{j=1}^{N_p}$ from the stage prior; set $\beta_0\leftarrow 0$
\WHILE{$\beta_m < 1$}
    \STATE Find $\beta_{m+1}$ s.t.\ $\mathrm{CoV}[\{w_j\}]=\delta_{\mathrm{target}}$ via bisection
    \STATE Compute weights $w_j \leftarrow p(\yobs\mid\btheta_j)^{\beta_{m+1}-\beta_m}$
    \STATE Resample $N_p$ particles $\propto w_j$ (systematic)
    \STATE Estimate weighted covariance $\hat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}^{(m)}$ (weighted particle covariance)
    \FOR{$k = 1$ \TO $n_{\mathrm{mut}}$}
        \STATE Propose $\btheta_{j}'$ for all $j$ (random-walk or differential-evolution move)
        \STATE Evaluate all $N_p$ likelihoods in parallel via \texttt{jax.vmap} on GPU
        \STATE Accept or reject (Metropolis--Hastings); reject proposals outside the box
    \ENDFOR
\ENDWHILE
\STATE $K\leftarrow m$ (number of tempering steps)
\STATE Record MAP and posterior standard deviation (SD) per parameter
\STATE $\triangleright$ \textbf{Stage (ii): composition likelihood, box mean $\pm4$\,SD}
\STATE Repeat lines 2--14 on the narrowed box, starting from the previous particles
\STATE $\triangleright$ \textbf{Stages (iii)--(iv): add the viability channel; initial box, then mean $\pm4$\,SD}
\STATE Augment likelihood with the weighted viability channel (Table~\ref{tab:weights})
\STATE Repeat lines 2--14 for each stage
\RETURN $\hat{\btheta}^{\mathrm{MAP}}$, posterior samples, $\ln\hat{Z}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Identifiability of the interaction entries}
\label{sec:S_ident}
Table~\ref{tab:S_ident} lists, for the final stage (iv) of every condition (\TBD{DS, DH: stage (iii)}), the median and the 90\% interval of each entry, pooled over the three seeds.
The reference box is the prior box of that stage (Table~\ref{tab:S_boxes}, column (iv); for DS and DH column (iii)).
An entry whose pooled posterior SD is at least 0.8 times that of a uniform distribution on this box is shown in grey and excluded from the seed-agreement criterion: the data do not constrain its magnitude beyond the prior box. Among these, entries whose 90\% interval excludes zero are marked s.o.\ (sign only), the others n.i.\ (not identified). All other entries are called identified (constrained by the data); an identified entry need not be sign-determined (e.g.\ $a_{45}$ in DS and DH).
Where the stage-(iv) box was narrowed to the stage-(iii) mean $\pm4$\,SD, the uniform SD on that box is about 2.3 stage-(iii) SDs; for these entries the classification is in effect made against the stage-(iii) box.

The threshold 0.8 is a convention. Table~\ref{tab:S_thresh} counts the identified entries for thresholds between 0.5 and 0.9.
The counts change, most in the commensal conditions, where some entries lie close to 0.8 (CS: 0.77 and 0.81; CH: 0.73 and 0.83), whereas DS has no entry between 0.71 and 0.93.
The pattern does not change: at every threshold the dysbiotic conditions have at least as many identified entries as the commensal ones, and the entries identified in the commensal conditions are interactions of So, An and Vei; at 0.9 they also include So--Fn and So--Pg, the two entries that are sign-determined in CH.

\paragraph{Curvature of the likelihood.}
As a check that does not use the posterior samples, we computed the Hessian $\mathbf{H}$ of $-\ln p(\yobs\mid\btheta)$ at the MAP over the 15 free entries, scaled each entry by the standard deviation of a uniform distribution on its box, and counted the eigen-directions with scaled curvature of at least $1/0.8^2$, i.e.\ directions in which the likelihood is narrower than the identification threshold.
Their number is 4, 4, 8 and 5 in CS, CH, DS and DH, against 4, 6, 8 and 9 entries identified from the posterior\TBD{prov.}.
The counts agree in CS and DS; in CH and DH the posterior identifies more entries than the local curvature, because several entries are constrained jointly along correlated directions and by the non-quadratic shape of the likelihood, which a single Hessian at the MAP does not capture.
The MAP is the best particle and not an exact optimum (4--5 small negative eigenvalues), so entry-wise Laplace intervals are not reported.

\label{sec:S_total}

\section{Composition-only fit in CS}
\label{sec:S_ceiling}
To separate a limitation of the model from the conflict between the two likelihood channels in CS, we minimised the composition error alone (unweighted mean squared difference of the normalised fractions; viability channel, likelihood weights and prior omitted) with a gradient-based local optimiser (L-BFGS-B), started from the final MAP and from two random perturbations of it, with every entry restricted to $[-15,20]$.
The best solution reaches a composition RMSE of 0.075 (per species 0.13, 0.04, 0.10, 0.005 and 0.005 for So, An, Vei, Fn and Pg), against 0.179 at the MAP, but its community live fraction is 0.97 at every time point, against the measured 0.65--0.77.
Maximising the full likelihood in the same way from the MAP lowers the composition RMSE only to 0.166.
The model can therefore follow the rapid rise of So between day~1 and day~3, but not together with the measured live fraction.
This is a deterministic diagnostic, not a posterior; its solution lies partly outside the prior box (e.g.\ $a_{13}=20$).

\section{Growth-induced stress at a constrained material point}
\label{sec:S_growth}
To show how the calibration enters a mechanical model, the posterior dynamics of each condition drive the isotropic growth law of the continuum model of \citet{Klempt2024ContinuumGrowth}: $\mathbf{F}_g=(1+\alpha)\mathbf{I}$ with $\mathrm{d}\alpha/\mathrm{d}T^\ast=k_\alpha\varphi_g$, where $T^\ast$ is time normalised by the length of the experiment (21 days) and $k_\alpha=10^{-3}$ as in that work.
At a material point that is constrained in all directions, $\mathbf{F}=\mathbf{I}$, the elastic part is $\mathbf{F}_e=\mathbf{I}/(1+\alpha)$ with $J_e=(1+\alpha)^{-3}$, the deviatoric stress vanishes and the Cauchy stress is $\boldsymbol{\sigma}=K(J_e-1)\mathbf{I}$, with $K=E/[3(1-2\nu)]$, $E=E_0(\varphi_{\mathrm{tot}}^2+f)$, $\nu=0.49$ and $f=10^{-3}$, so no finite-element solution is needed.
We report the growth pressure $p=-\tfrac13\operatorname{tr}\boldsymbol{\sigma}$ relative to $E_0$ for two choices of the driver $\varphi_g$: the living volume $\sum_i\varphi_i\psi_i$, whose community fraction is constrained by the live/dead data, and the total volume $\varphi_{\mathrm{tot}}=\sum_i\varphi_i$, which the likelihood does not constrain (it uses fractions only).
Driven by the total volume, the four conditions are indistinguishable: $\varphi_{\mathrm{tot}}$ stays at 0.95--0.97 and $p/E_0$ on day~21 is 0.042--0.046 in all of them (Fig.~\ref{fig:S_growth}b, dashed).
Driven by the living volume, they separate: $p/E_0$ on day~21 is 0.020 (90\% interval $[0.017,0.023]$) in CH, 0.024 ($[0.021,0.027]$) in DS and DH, and 0.041 ($[0.032,0.044]$; MAP 0.030) in CS\TBD{prov.}.
The high CS value follows the bulk of the CS posterior, whose live fraction (0.92 on day~21) lies above the measured 0.65--0.77 (Section~\ref{sec:S_ceiling}); the CS MAP, which matches the live fraction better, gives 0.030.
This is an illustration of the coupling, not a prediction of the stress in a biofilm: $k_\alpha$ and $E_0$ are taken from another system and $p$ scales linearly with both, and a spatial model adds nutrient transport, boundary conditions and the spatial distribution of the species.
It shows that the dependence of the growth stress on the condition enters through the calibrated viability rather than through assumed species-specific growth parameters, and that the posterior spread can be propagated to the stress.
